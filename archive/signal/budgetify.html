01 — THE PROBLEM

The data is already on your phone.

Most people in India get a text for every bank transaction — UPI, card, ATM, NEFT. Yet the popular budgeting apps still ask for the opposite of what that makes possible.

They upload your financial life to the cloud to “sync” it.

They ask you to log every expense by hand — which nobody keeps up.

They bury the experience in ads, upsells and sign-ups.

They stop working the moment you lose signal.

02 — THE APPROACH

Three decisions that shaped everything else.

A

Parse templates, not guesses

TRAI's DLT rules make banks register their SMS templates, so each bank's formats are a small, stable set. Banks get anchored template packs; the generic cascade only runs as a fallback, and its output is graded lower-confidence.

B

Privacy by architecture

The shipping app doesn't request internet access, so it is technically incapable of uploading your data. Backups are AES-256 encrypted files that you keep wherever you like.

C

Admit uncertainty

Low-confidence entries go to Tidy up: one card at a time, three clear answers. Each correction teaches the parser, so the same format lands right next time.

“A budgeting app that quietly gets things wrong is worse than one that admits uncertainty.”

03 — WHAT SHIPPED

Far more than a spending list.

Recurring payments

Subscriptions, rent and EMIs, auto-linked to the debit that pays them.

Tax organiser

Tag spends into 80C, 80D, 24(b) and HRA, and watch each section fill toward its cap.

Offline splits

A split ledger where the other people are names, not accounts.

Statement import

CSV and Excel from any bank, with duplicate detection against the SMS copy.

Monthly Wrapped

A shareable recap poster that hides amounts unless you choose to reveal them.

Gamified budgets

Streaks, freezes and a reward road that unlocks themes and launcher icons.

04 — ENGINEERING NOTES

Small calls that build trust.

A money coach that stays quiet

Spending is heavily skewed, and one big purchase wrecks an average. Nudges use the median and median absolute deviation, so a lone outlier can't set off false alarms.

Removal that asks what you meant

“Not a transaction” mutes that message shape from that sender; “Just this one” removes a single entry. Both can be undone, and the undo survives an app restart.

Built to be usable

Screen-reader labels on the transaction list, long-press as an alternative to swiping, and layouts that hold up at large text sizes in all six languages.
