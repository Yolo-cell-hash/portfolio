01 · What makes it different

Smart about your data. Blind to it, too.

Everything clever in Vaulty happens on the phone, so it can understand your life without ever seeing it.

01

Capture that understands you

Type naturally — “Car insurance expires Nov 12, Policy #9812” — and an on-device parser pulls out the title, category, expiry date and details. “Mom's shoe size” links itself to Mom, and #car becomes a tag.

02

Or just snap it

On-device text recognition reads a document, card or label. The photo is encrypted and never uploaded.

03

An Expiry Radar

A grid and a list sorted by urgency, with countdowns. Colour stays neutral until it needs you: amber when soon, red when overdue.

04

Dates that repeat properly

Birthdays and subscriptions count down to the next one and never show as “expired”. “Renewed it” resets a passport or policy in one tap.

05

Ask your vault

Natural-language search, backed by a full-text index inside the encrypted database.

06

Zero-trust by default

An SQLCipher AES-256 database and AES-GCM photos, with keys held in the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore. Face ID or fingerprint lock, sensitive items blurred until you verify, a privacy cover in the app switcher, and an auto-lock after 20 seconds. Backups are sealed with your passphrase, and release builds don't request internet access at all.

07

Reminders that keep secrets

Scheduled locally. A notification says what's due and when, never the details stored inside it.

02 · Design system

“Editorial Vault.”

Premium and calm by default, with a hint of Gen Z in a few deliberate places. I wrote the system before drawing a single screen.

Paper, ink, one violet, a little lime

Ask anything.

Instrument Serif for display, Instrument Sans for UI, and DM Mono for anything you'd copy: UK 5 #9812

Eight rules, enforced in review

  • No emoji, gradients or drop shadows — custom glyphs and 1px hairlines instead.
  • Don't put an icon in a box by default; a coloured glyph and a small-caps label do the job.
  • Italic serif marks exactly one word per headline.
  • Lime is rare, and never used for text on paper.
  • Colour means something: countdowns stay neutral until they need you.
  • Copyable data is always monospace.
  • Every text colour clears 4.5:1 contrast in both themes.
  • Vo, the mascot, appears only at emotional moments: onboarding, empty states, the lock screen.

03 · How I'm building it

Decide the hard things first.

Budgetify taught me that privacy and polish are cheapest at the start. Vaulty began with the design system and the threat model, and the features came after.

  1. Market read before pixels

    I studied how premium apps brand themselves in 2026 — type as the voice, one committed direction — and listed the tells of generic, templated UI so Vaulty would avoid every one.

    A written design rationale

  2. Design system first

    Tokens, a type scale, a custom glyph set and the eight rules came before any screen. The design doc is the source of truth, and code review checks against it.

    8 rules · own icon set · 2 themes

  3. Privacy designed in

    I mapped what's stored, where, and how it's protected before writing storage code. The privacy policy was written alongside the app, and each permission is asked only at the moment it's needed.

    No internet permission in release

  4. Native where it matters

    One Flutter codebase, but system pieces take each platform's form: iOS gets its own alerts, action sheets, date wheels, stacked sheets, swipe actions and haptics.

    Platform-true on iOS and Android

  5. Adaptive and accessible

    Phones stay portrait. Tablets, foldables and ChromeOS keep a centred 600dp column, and every colour token is contrast-checked in light and dark.

    WCAG AA in both themes

  6. Store-ready details

    Launcher icons rendered from the mascot painter, including iOS 18 dark and tinted variants, a native splash that hands off to a skippable intro, and store privacy labels written to match.

    Coming soon to both stores
